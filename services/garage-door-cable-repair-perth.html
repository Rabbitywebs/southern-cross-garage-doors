Signs your garage door cable has snapped

  • The door hangs lower on one side, or sits crooked in the opening
  • A loose or tangled cable near the top corner of the door
  • Frayed, rusty or unravelling cable near the bottom bracket
  • The door jams halfway or jerks when it moves
  • A loud snap, followed by the door dropping or stopping suddenly

Why cables fail in Perth

Lift cables carry the door's weight every time it opens and closes, so they slowly wear and fray. In coastal suburbs like Cottesloe, Scarborough, Mandurah and Rockingham, salty air rusts the steel strands much faster. A worn spring or a door that has come off its track can also put extra strain on a cable until it snaps.

How we repair snapped cables

  • Secure the door so it can't drop or swing while we work
  • Inspect the door and give you a fixed price to approve
  • Replace both cables, so the door lifts evenly on each side
  • Check and re-seat the cable drums, bottom brackets and rollers
  • Re-balance the door and test the opener's safety settings

How much does cable replacement cost?

It depends on your door type and size, and on whether anything else was damaged when the cable snapped. We inspect the door and give you a fixed price to approve before any work starts, with no call-out fee in Perth metro.

Is it safe to use the door with a snapped cable?

No. With one cable gone, the door can twist, jam or fall. Keep people and cars clear, don't force it open or closed, disconnect the opener if you can, and call a technician.

Cable repair FAQ

How long does a cable replacement take?

Usually about an hour once we're on site, a little longer if the door has come off its track too.

Should both cables be replaced?

Yes. Both cables wear at the same rate, so if one has snapped the other is usually close behind. Replacing both keeps the door lifting evenly.

My car is stuck inside. What should I do?

Don't try to force the door. Call us and let us know your car is stuck, and we'll prioritise your job.
